# Product principles

These principles describe durable product intent. They should guide design choices without prescribing implementation details.

## Optimize for understanding and mastery

Completion is evidence of activity, not necessarily learning. Prefer experiences that reveal reasoning, correct misconceptions, reconnect missing prerequisites, and help knowledge endure.

## Meet the learner at the right point

Difficulty should be responsive but purposeful. When a prerequisite gap blocks progress, help the learner repair it and reconnect the work to their original goal. Adaptation must remain explainable and should not trap a learner in endlessly easier material.

## Make mathematics visible and interactive

Use visual models and direct manipulation when they clarify a relationship or provide a meaningful way to answer. Do not add animation or interaction that distracts from the mathematical idea.

## Encourage without patronizing

Use a calm, modern visual language suitable for teenagers and adults as well as younger learners. Celebrate effort, consistency, improvement, and mastery. Avoid childish decoration, shame, manipulative streaks, and global leaderboards that disadvantage learners who already struggle.

## Earn trust

Questions, generated values, accepted answers, explanations, and progress signals must be mathematically reliable. Prefer deterministic behavior that can be reproduced, tested, and explained.

## Design for different ways of participating

An answer is not always a text field. Learners may type an expression, choose an option, place a point, order objects, manipulate a graph, or construct geometry. Interaction design is part of pedagogy.

## Include from the beginning

Keyboard access, screen-reader support, semantic mathematics, contrast, color independence, reduced motion, responsive layouts, and varied learning needs are requirements, not later polish.

## Respect learners

Collect as little personal data as practical. Favor anonymous use where it can meet the need, require accounts only for clear benefits, and never base the product on advertising profiles of children.

## Build for many contexts

Separate mathematical skills from curricula, grade levels, countries, and display languages. Begin with a real learner and an initial curriculum, but do not make either the universal domain model.

## Grow through open contribution

Make content understandable and verifiable outside application code. Provide schemas, examples, documentation, and automated checks so that educators and translators can contribute without having to become product engineers.
