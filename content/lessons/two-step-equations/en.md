## Two moves you know {#moves}

In “Equations and the balance” you used two moves that keep the scale level:

- Take the same off both pans, as in $x + 3 = 7$.
- Share each side into equal groups, as in $3x = 12$.

Now one equation needs both moves.

## Both moves on the balance {#balance}

In $2x + 3 = 11$, the left pan holds two boxes of $x$ and 3 weights. The right pan holds 11 weights, and the scale is level.

First take the loose weights off both pans, one at a time, until the boxes stand alone. Then share the weights on the right into 2 equal groups, one group for each box.

:::visual{type="balance-two-step-explorer" a="2" c="3" b="11"}
:::

## Take away first, then share {#order}

**First take away the loose weights, then share.** Then you share only what balances the boxes. Sharing first is harder: the loose weights would have to be shared too, and 3 weights do not split into 2 equal groups of whole weights.

:::example{title="Worked example: 3x + 2 = 14" id="example"}
Solve $3x + 2 = 14$.

1. Take 2 away from both sides. The boxes stand alone: $3x = 12$.
2. Share both sides into 3 equal groups. One box balances one group: $x = 4$.
3. Check: put 4 in place of $x$. $3 · 4 + 2 = 14$, so the answer is right.
:::

## Try it yourself {#try}

Solve these with the same two steps:

- $2x + 1 = 7$
- $3x + 4 = 10$
- $2x + 5 = 15$

## Check your answer {#check}

Put your answer in place of $x$. If the left side gives the number on the right, the scale is level and the answer is right.

## Practise {#practice}

Solve equations of your own. If you get stuck, ask for help, and the balance shows you both steps.
