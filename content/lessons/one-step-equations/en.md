## What is an equation? {#what}

An **equation** says that two things are equal. In $x + 3 = 7$, the letter $x$ stands for a number we don’t know yet. **Solving** the equation means finding that number.

You can think of an equation as a **balance scale**. The left side goes in one pan and the right side in the other. The equals sign means the scale is level.

## Keep the scale level {#balance}

Here the box holds $x$. Next to it are 3 weights of 1, and in the other pan are 7 weights of 1. The scale is level, so $x + 3 = 7$.

Take weights off and watch the scale. If you take one off only one pan, the scale tips. If you take the same off both pans, it stays level.

:::visual{type="balance-explorer" a="3" b="7"}
:::

## The rule {#rule}

**Whatever you do to one side, do the same to the other.** Then the scale stays level, and the equation stays true.

To get $x$ on its own, take away what is next to it, from both sides.

:::example{title="Worked example: x + 3 = 7" id="example"}
1. $x$ has 3 next to it. Take 3 away from both sides.
2. On the left, only $x$ is left. On the right, 7 take away 3 leaves 4.
3. Check: put 4 in place of $x$. $4 + 3 = 7$, so the answer is right.

$$x + 3 − 3 = 7 − 3$$

$$x = 4$$
:::

## Try it yourself {#try}

Solve this one with the scale. How many weights must you take off each pan to leave $x$ on its own?

:::visual{type="balance-explorer" a="5" b="12"}
:::

Then try these in your head:

- $x + 2 = 9$
- $x + 6 = 10$
- $x + 4 = 13$

## Check your answer {#check}

You can always check an answer by putting it in place of $x$. If both sides give the same number, it is right. If they don’t, the scale would tip, so try again.
