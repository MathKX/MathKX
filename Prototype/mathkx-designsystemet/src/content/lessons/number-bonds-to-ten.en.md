## What are number bonds to ten? {#what}

**Number bonds to ten** are pairs of numbers that add up to 10. For example, 3 and 7 are a number bond to ten, because $3 + 7 = 10$.

It’s a common term in schools, but it isn’t a new kind of arithmetic. It is simply a name for pairs of numbers that belong together because they fill up a ten. In Norwegian they are called *tiervenner*, “friends of ten”.

## All the pairs {#pairs}

There are only six pairs, so they are possible to learn:

| Pair | Sum |
| 0 and 10 | $0 + 10 = 10$ |
| 1 and 9 | $1 + 9 = 10$ |
| 2 and 8 | $2 + 8 = 10$ |
| 3 and 7 | $3 + 7 = 10$ |
| 4 and 6 | $4 + 6 = 10$ |
| 5 and 5 | $5 + 5 = 10$ |

The order doesn’t matter: $9 + 1$ gives the same as $1 + 9$.

## Why is this useful? {#why}

It’s easy to keep counting from 10. If you fill up to 10 first, the rest of the calculation gets simpler.

:::example Example: 8 + 5 {#example}
1. 8 needs 2 to make 10. (8 and 2 are a number bond to ten.)
2. Split 5 into 2 and 3.
3. Add 2 to 8. That makes 10, with 3 left over.

$$8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13$$
:::

## Try it in your head {#try}

- $9 + 4$: 9 needs 1. Split 4 into 1 and 3. That makes $10 + 3$.
- $7 + 6$: 7 needs 3. Split 6 into 3 and 3. That makes $10 + 3$.
- $6 + 5$: What is the partner of 6? How much of 5 is left?

You don’t always have to go via 10. If you see the answer another way, that is just as correct.
